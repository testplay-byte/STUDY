Example 9.39.

Two cards are drawn from a well-shuffled ordinary pack of 52 cards. The first card is replaced before selecting the second card. Find the following probabilities.

(i) both are kings (ii) first is king and second is queen (iii) one is king and one is queen (iv) both are faced cards (v) first is card of diamonds and the second is an ace.

Solution: We shall assume here that all the 52 cards are equally likely and each card has the probability of $1/52$. As the first card is returned to the main pack before selecting the second card, therefore the selection of the second card is independent of the first card. Thus the two cards are selected independently.

(i) Both are kings (There are four kings in a playing card)

$\text{P( first card is a king )} = \text{P(K}_1) = \left( \frac{4}{52} \right)$ and $\text{P( second card is a king )} = \text{P(K}_2) = \left( \frac{4}{52} \right)$

Since K$_1$ and K$_2$ are independent, therefore

$\text{P( both are kings )} = \text{P(K}_1 \cap \text{K}_2) = \text{P(K}_1) \text{ P(K}_2) = \left( \frac{4}{52} \right) \left( \frac{4}{52} \right) = \frac{1}{169}$

(ii) First is king and second is queen (There are four kings and four queens)

$\text{P( first is king )} = \text{P(K}_1) = \frac{4}{52}$ and $\text{P( second is queen )} = \text{P(Q}_2) = \frac{4}{52}$

Since K$_1$ and Q$_2$ are independent, therefore

$\text{P(K}_1 \cap \text{Q}_2) = \text{P(K}_1) \text{ P(Q}_2) = \left( \frac{4}{52} \right) \left( \frac{4}{52} \right) = \frac{1}{169}$

(iii) One is king and one is queen (There are four kings and four queens)

$\text{P( first is king )} = \text{P(K}_1) = \frac{4}{52}$ and $\text{P( second is queen )} = \text{P(Q}_2) = \frac{4}{52}$

Since K$_1$ and Q$_2$ are independent, therefore

$\text{P(K}_1 \cap \text{Q}_2) = \text{P(K}_1) \text{ P(Q}_2) = \left( \frac{4}{52} \right) \left( \frac{1}{52} \right) = \frac{1}{169}$
