Example 9.42.

In a certain teaching department, there are 4 lecturers and 4 assistant professors. Any three teachers are called upon to give a lecture turn by turn. Find the probability that a lecturer will come first and then the two assistant professors will come.

Solution: Let A represent the event that first to deliver lecture is a lecturer, B represent the event that the second teacher is assistant professor and let C represent the event that the third teacher is also assistant professor. We have to calculate the probability of the joint event $\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B} \cap \mathrm{C}$ by the multiplication rule

$$\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B} \cap \mathrm{C})=\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A}) \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B} / \mathrm{A}) \mathrm{P}(\mathrm{C} / \mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B})$$

Here, $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A})=4 / 8$, $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{B} / \mathrm{A})=4 / 7$ and $\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{C} / \mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B})=3 / 6$. Therefore

$$\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{A} \cap \mathrm{B} \cap \mathrm{C})=\left(\frac{4}{8}\right)\left(\frac{4}{7}\right)\left(\frac{3}{6}\right)=\frac{1}{7}$$
