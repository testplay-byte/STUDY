## 1.11.3 Graph of $y = \sec\theta$ and $y = \cosec\theta$

Domain and range of $y = \sec\theta$ and $y = \cosec\theta$ is obvious form the graphs of both functions shown below.

[Figure F3]

| Function | $y = \sec\theta$ |
| :--- | :--- |
| Domain | $\theta \neq (2n + 1)\frac{\pi}{2}; n \in Z$ |
| Range | $y \leq -1, y \geq 1$ or $y \in (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty)$ |

[Figure F4]

| Function | $y = \csc\theta$ |
| :--- | :--- |
| Domain | $\theta \neq n\pi; n \in Z$ |
| Range | $y \leq -1, y \geq 1$ or $y \in (-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty)$ |

## Figures on this page

### Figure F1 — Graph of y = tanθ (top right)
- **Type:** curve-plot
- **Caption/Number:** (none printed)
- **Description:** A Cartesian coordinate system with horizontal axis labeled with multiples of $\pi$ ($-2\pi, -\pi, 0, \pi, 2\pi$) and vertical axis labeled with integers ($-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3$). The graph displays multiple branches of a green curve representing the tangent function. Each branch is strictly increasing and passes through the origin $(0,0)$ or its periodic equivalents. Vertical asymptotes are present at odd multiples of $\pi/2$. The curve approaches positive infinity from the left of an asymptote and negative infinity from the right.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Illustrates that $y = \tan\theta$ has vertical asymptotes at $\theta = (2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2}$, a period of $\pi$, and a range of all real numbers.

### Figure F2 — Graph of y = cotθ (middle right)
- **Type:** curve-plot
- **Caption/Number:** (none printed)
- **Description:** A Cartesian coordinate system with horizontal axis labeled with multiples of $\pi$ ($-2\pi, -\pi, 0, \pi, 2\pi$) and vertical axis labeled with integers ($-2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3$). The graph displays multiple branches of a dark blue/purple curve representing the cotangent function. Each branch is strictly decreasing. Vertical asymptotes are present at integer multiples of $\pi$ (including at $0$). The curve approaches negative infinity from the left of an asymptote and positive infinity from the right.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Illustrates that $y = \cot\theta$ has vertical asymptotes at $\theta = n\pi$, a period of $\pi$, and a range of all real numbers.

### Figure F3 — Graphs of y = secθ (bottom left)
- **Type:** curve-plot
- **Caption/Number:** (none printed)
- **Description:** Two separate Cartesian plots stacked vertically sharing the same horizontal axis scale (multiples of $\pi$: $-2\pi, -\pi, 0, \pi, 2\pi$).
    *   *Top plot:* Shows green U-shaped curves opening upwards. The minimum points of these curves touch the line $y=1$ at even multiples of $\pi$. There are gaps between the curves where vertical asymptotes occur.
    *   *Bottom plot:* Shows green inverted U-shaped curves (arches) opening downwards. The maximum points of these curves touch the line $y=-1$ at odd multiples of $\pi$.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Demonstrates that $y = \sec\theta$ has a range of $(-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty)$ and vertical asymptotes where $\cos\theta = 0$.

### Figure F4 — Graphs of y = cosecθ (bottom right)
- **Type:** curve-plot
- **Caption/Number:** (none printed)
- **Description:** Two separate Cartesian plots stacked vertically sharing the same horizontal axis scale (multiples of $\pi$: $-2\pi, -\pi, 0, \pi, 2\pi$).
    *   *Top plot:* Shows green U-shaped curves opening upwards. The minimum points touch the line $y=1$ at values corresponding to $\pi/2 + 2n\pi$.
    *   *Bottom plot:* Shows green inverted U-shaped curves (arches) opening downwards. The maximum points touch the line $y=-1$ at values corresponding to $-\pi/2 + 2n\pi$ (or $3\pi/2 + 2n\pi$).
- **Mathematical meaning:** Demonstrates that $y = \csc\theta$ has a range of $(-\infty, -1] \cup [1, \infty)$ and vertical asymptotes where $\sin\theta = 0$ (at $n\pi$).
