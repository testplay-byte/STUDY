## 2.2 Continuity

In the case of limit, we have used the phrase “connect the points with smooth curve”. The phrase provides the concept of graph that is a nice continuous curve that is, a curve with no gaps or breaks. Indeed, a continuous function is often described as one whose graph can be drawn without lifting pencil from paper.

Before moving towards the precise definition of continuity, we demonstrate in figures some intuitive examples of functions that are not continuous or continuous at a number.

### Figure F3 — Discontinuity example Fig (i) (middle left)
- **Type:** curve-plot
- **Caption/Number:** Fig (i)
- **Description:** A graph showing a curve approaching a vertical asymptote (dotted line) at $x=1$. The curve goes to $-\infty$ from the left and $+\infty$ from the right. Text below states: "$\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$ does not exist and $f(1)$ is not defined." The word "(Discontinuous)" is annotated near the asymptote.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Demonstrates an infinite discontinuity where the limit does not exist and the function value is undefined.

### Figure F4 — Discontinuity example Fig (ii) (middle right)
- **Type:** curve-plot
- **Caption/Number:** Fig (ii)
- **Description:** A graph showing a jump discontinuity at $x=1$. An open circle is on the upper branch and a solid dot is on the lower branch. Text below states: "$\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$ does not exist and $f(1)$ is defined." The word "(Discontinuous)" is annotated near the gap.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Demonstrates a jump discontinuity where the limit does not exist but the function has a defined value at the point.

### Figure F5 — Discontinuity example Fig (iii) (bottom left)
- **Type:** line-graph
- **Caption/Number:** Fig (iii)
- **Description:** A straight line with a slope, featuring an open circle (hole) at $x=1$. Text below states: "$\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$ exist but $f(1)$ is not defined." The word "(Discontinuous)" is annotated near the hole.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Demonstrates a removable discontinuity where the limit exists but the function is undefined at that point.

### Figure F6 — Discontinuity example Fig (iv) (bottom center)
- **Type:** line-graph
- **Caption/Number:** Fig (iv)
- **Description:** A straight line with an open circle at one y-value and a solid dot at a different y-value, both at $x=1$. Text below states: "$\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$ exist and $f(1)$ is defined but $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) \neq f(1)$". The word "(Discontinuous)" is annotated near the points.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Demonstrates a removable discontinuity where both the limit and function value exist but are not equal.

### Figure F7 — Continuous example Fig (v) (bottom right)
- **Type:** line-graph
- **Caption/Number:** Fig (v)
- **Description:** A continuous straight line passing through a solid dot at $x=1$ without any breaks or holes. Text below states: "$\lim_{x \to 1} f(x)$ exist and $f(1)$ is defined and $\lim_{x \to 1} f(x) = f(1)$". The word "(Continuous)" is annotated on the graph.
- **Mathematical meaning:** Demonstrates continuity at a point where the limit exists, the function is defined, and they are equal.
