# Page 17 — Unit 02: Limit, Continuity and Derivative

> 📄 Original scan: [0017.jpg](../../../Raw/Mathematics/Unit-02-Limit-Continuity-and-Derivative/0017.jpg) · printed page 59

**Example 17:** Find the derivative of the following w.r.t. $x$.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;a. $y = (x+1)^2$ &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;b. $y = (x+1)(x-2)$ &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;c. $y = \frac{x^3+x^2}{3x}$

**Solution:**

a. $y = (x+1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + 2x + 1] = \frac{d}{dx}(x^2) + 2\frac{d}{dx}(x) + \frac{d}{dx}(1) = 2x + 2 + 0 = 2x + 2$$

b. $y = (x+1)(x-2) = x^2 - x - 2$ &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;c. $y = \frac{x^3+x^2}{3x} = \frac{x^3}{3x} + \frac{x^2}{3x}$

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{d}{dx}x^2 - \frac{d}{dx}x - \frac{d}{dx}2 \\
&= 2x - 1 - 0 = 2x - 1
\end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{x^2}{3} + \frac{x}{3} \\
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dx}x^2 + \frac{1}{3}\frac{d}{dx}x = \frac{1}{3}(2x) + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{3}x + \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}$$

**Note:** In the different contents of science, engineering and business functions are often expressed in variable other than $x$ and $y$. Correspondingly, we must adapt the derivative notation to new symbols, for example:

| Function | Derivative | Function | Derivative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $V(t) = 4t$ | $V'(t) = \frac{dV}{dt} = 4$ | $H(z) = \frac{1}{4}z^6$ | $H'(z) = \frac{dH}{dz} = \frac{3}{2}z^5$ |
| $A(r) = \pi r^2$ | $A'(r) = \frac{dA}{dr} = 2 \pi r$ | $r(\theta) = 4\theta^3 - 3\theta$ | $r'(\theta) = \frac{dr}{d\theta} = 12\theta^2 - 3$ |
