## Theorem 3.3:

If $f$ and $g$ are integrable on $[a, b]$ and $c$ is a constant, then $cf$, $f + g$ and $f - g$ are integrable on $[a, b]$ and the following statements are true.

(i) $\displaystyle \int_{a}^{b} c\ f(x)dx = c \int_{a}^{b} f(x)dx$ (The constant has no effects of limits on it.)

(ii) $\displaystyle \int_{a}^{b}[f(x) \pm g(x)]dx = \int_{a}^{b} f(x)dx \pm \int_{a}^{b} g(x)dx$

Showing that the limit of a sum or difference is the sum or difference of the limits.

(iii) $\displaystyle \int_{a}^{b}[c\ f(x) \pm d\ g(x)]dx = c \int_{a}^{b} f(x)dx \pm d \int_{a}^{b} g(x)dx$

## Example 22: Find:

(i) $\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{4}[2f(x) + 5g(x)]dx$ if $\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{4} f(x)dx = 2$ and $\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{4} g(x)dx = 4$

(ii) $\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{3} 4f(x)dx$ if $\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{2} f(x)dx = 3$ and $\displaystyle \int_{2}^{3} f(x)dx = 1$

## Solution:

(i) $\begin{aligned} \int_{-1}^{4}[2f(x) + 5g(x)]dx &= \int_{-1}^{4} 2f(x)dx + \int_{-1}^{4} 5g(x)dx = 2 \int_{-1}^{4} f(x)dx + 5 \int_{-1}^{4} g(x)dx \\ &= 2(2) + 5(4) = 24 \end{aligned}$

(ii) $\begin{aligned} \int_{-1}^{3} 4f(x)dx &= 4 \int_{-1}^{3} f(x)dx = 4 \left[\int_{-1}^{2} f(x)dx + \int_{2}^{3} f(x)dx\right] \\ &= 4(3 + 1) = 4 \times 4 = 16 \end{aligned}$
