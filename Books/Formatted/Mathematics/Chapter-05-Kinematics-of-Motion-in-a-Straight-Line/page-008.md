## 5.3 Velocity as Derivative of Displacement Function

Let a particle be moving and its position can be determined by the displacement function $S$. By definition velocity is the time rate of change of displacement. So we may write:

$$v = \text{velocity} = \frac{dS}{dt}$$

This gives us the instantaneous velocity of the particle at time '$t$'. For a particular value of '$t$' we will get the velocity of the particle at that particular time.

**Example 2:** A particle is moving such that its position can be determined by the function $S = t^2 + 2sint$. Find its velocity at any time '$t$'. Also find its initial velocity and velocity at time $t = \frac{\pi}{3}$ ($S$ is in meters and $t$ is in seconds).

**Solution:**

$$S = t^2 + 2sint$$
