# Page 25 — Unit 07: Conic Section

> 📄 Original scan: [0025.jpg](../../../Raw/Mathematics/Unit-07-Conic-Section/0025.jpg) · printed page 206

**Example 19:** Convert the equation of ellipse $4x^2 + 9y^2 + 8x - 36y + 4 = 0$ in standard form. Also find its elements and draw the graph.

**Solution:** Given equation of ellipse is:
$$\begin{aligned}
4x^2 + 9y^2 + 8x - 36y + 4 = 0 & \quad \Rightarrow \quad (4x^2 + 8x) + (9y^2 - 36y) = -4 \\
\Rightarrow 4(x^2 + 2x) + 9(y^2 - 4y) = -4 \\
\Rightarrow 4[x^2 + 2x + 1 - 1] + 9[y^2 - 4y + 4 - 4] = -4 \\
\Rightarrow 4(x + 1)^2 - 4 + 9(y - 2)^2 - 36 = -4 & \quad \text{or} \quad 4(x + 1)^2 + 9(y - 2)^2 = 36
\end{aligned}$$

Dividing both sides by 36, we get:

$$\frac{(x+1)^2}{9} + \frac{(y-2)^2}{4} = 1 \qquad \text{or} \qquad \frac{(x+1)^2}{3^2} + \frac{(y-2)^2}{2^2} = 1$$

Which is the standard form of the equation of ellipse.

Letting $x + 1 = X$ and $y - 2 = Y$, the equation becomes:

$$\frac{X^2}{3^2} + \frac{Y^2}{2^2} = 1$$

Its major axis is along $x$-axis. Hence $a = 3$ and $b = 2$.

Now we find its elements.

**Centre:** As the centre of ellipse is $(0,0)$, therefore $(X,Y) = (0,0)$

$$\begin{array}{lll}
\Rightarrow X = 0 & & Y = 0 \\
\Rightarrow x + 1 = 0 & & y - 2 = 0 \\
\Rightarrow x = -1 & & y = 2
\end{array}$$

Thus, the centre of ellipse is at $(-1,2)$.

**Foci:** As we know that $c = \pm\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ hence for the given equation $c = \pm\sqrt{9 - 4} = \pm\sqrt{5}$ therefore foci are $\text{F}_1(-c, 0) = (-\sqrt{5}, 0)$ and $\text{F}_2(-c, 0) = (-\sqrt{5}, 0)$.

**Major Axis:** As major axis of ellipse is along $x$-axis hence its equation is $Y = 0$
$\Rightarrow y - 2 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow y = 2$ is equation of major axis.

**Minor Axis:** Equation of minor axis is $X = 0 \quad \Rightarrow x + 1 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow x = -1$

**Vertices:** The vertices of given ellipse are $(-a, 0) = (-3, 0)$ and $(a, 0) = (3, 0)$.

**Co-Vertices:** Co-vertices of given ellipse are $(0, -b) = (0, -2)$ and $(0, b) = (0, 2)$.

**Directrices:** The equation of directrices is $X = \pm \frac{a^2}{c}$ i.e. $x + 1 = \pm \frac{9}{\sqrt{5}}$

$$\text{or } x = \pm \frac{9}{\sqrt{5}} - 1 = \frac{9-\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{-9-\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}}$$

$$x = \frac{-9 - \sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} \qquad x = \frac{-9 - \sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}}$$

The graph is shown in the adjoining figure.
