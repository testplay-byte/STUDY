# Page 30 — Unit 07: Conic Section

> 📄 Original scan: [0030.jpg](../../../Raw/Mathematics/Unit-07-Conic-Section/0030.jpg) · printed page 211

At point $(0,0)$
$$\Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{0-1}{2(0)+2} = \frac{1}{2}$$
is the slope of the tangent line at $P(0,0)$. Thus, equation of tangent line is
$$\begin{aligned} y-y_1 &= m(x-x_1) \\ \Rightarrow y-0 &= \frac{1}{2}(x-0) \Rightarrow 2y = x \quad \Rightarrow x-2y=0 \end{aligned}$$
Now slope of the normal line is $-\frac{1}{1/2}$. Thus, equation of normal line at point $P(0,0)$ is
$$\begin{aligned} y-0 &= -2(x-0) \Rightarrow y=-2x \\ &\quad 2x+y=0 \end{aligned}$$

**Alternatively**

Equation of ellipse is $x^2 + 2y^2 - 2x + 4y = 0$, convert it into standard form, i.e.;
$$\begin{aligned} &(x^2-2x)+2(y^2+2y)=0 \\ \Rightarrow &(x^2-2x+1-1)+2(y^2+2y+1-1)=0 \\ \Rightarrow &[(x-1)^2-1]+2[(y+1)^2-1]=0 \\ \Rightarrow &(x-1)^2-1+2(y+1)^2-2=0 \\ \Rightarrow &(x-1)^2+2(y+1)^2=3 \end{aligned}$$
Dividing both sides by 3
$$\begin{aligned} \Rightarrow &\frac{(x-1)^2}{3}+\frac{2(y+1)^2}{3}=1 \\ \Rightarrow &\frac{(x-1)^2}{3}+\frac{(y-(-1))^2}{3/2}=1 \end{aligned}$$
Compare it with standard equation $\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1; h=1; k=-1; a^2=3; b^2=3/2$

Equation of tangent line is
$$\frac{(x_1-h)(x-h)}{a^2} + \frac{(y_1-k)(y-k)}{b^2} = 1$$
Putting the values
$$\begin{aligned} &\frac{(0-1)(x-1)}{3}+\frac{(0-(-1))(y-(-1))}{3/2}=1 \\ \Rightarrow &\frac{-x+1}{3}+\frac{2(y+1)}{3}=1 \Rightarrow -x+1+2y+2=3 \Rightarrow -x+2y=0 \\ \Rightarrow &x-2y=0 \end{aligned}$$
And the equation of normal line is
$$a^2(y_1-k)(x-x_1)-b^2(x_1-h)(y-y_1)=0$$
Putting the values
$$\begin{aligned} \Rightarrow &3(0-(-1))(x-0)-3/2(0-1)(y-0)=0 \\ \Rightarrow &3x+\frac{3}{2}y=0 \\ \Rightarrow &2x+y=0, \text{ is the equation of normal line.} \end{aligned}$$
