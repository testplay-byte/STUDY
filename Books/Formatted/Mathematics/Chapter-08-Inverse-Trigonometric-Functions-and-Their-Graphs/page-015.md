## 8.2.1 Graph of $y = \text{Cos}^{-1} x$

The graph of the function $y = \text{Cos}^{-1} x$ represents the angle $y$ whose cosine is $x$. The domain of this function is $x \in [-1,1]$, and the range is $y \in [0,\pi]$. This means the output values $y$ will always be within this interval, corresponding to angles in radians from $0$ to $\pi$.

**Construction Steps:**
*   Draw the coordinate axes. Mark $x$-axis with values from $-1$ to $1$ and $y$-axis with values from $0$ to $\pi$.
*   Choose an appropriate scale.
*   Mark the points $(1, 0), \left(0.9, \frac{\pi}{6}\right), \left(0.5, \frac{\pi}{3}\right), \left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right), \left(-0.5, \frac{2\pi}{3}\right), \left(-0.9, \frac{5\pi}{6}\right), (-1, \pi)$ on the graph.
*   Connect these points with a smooth curve.

**Properties:**
*   **Domain and Range:** The domain is $[-1,1]$ and the range is $[0,\pi]$.
*   **Key Points:** $(1, 0), \left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}\right), (-1, \pi)$
*   **Appearance:** The graph appears as a smooth curve starting from $(1,0)$, decreasing through the key points, passes through the point $(1,0)$ and ending at $(-1, \pi)$.
*   **Monotonicity:** The graph is monotonically decreasing smoothly (it always decreases as $x$ increases).
*   **Neither even nor odd function**
*   **Reflection:** The graph of $y = \text{Cos}^{-1}x$, is a reflection of $y = \text{Cos } x$ across the line $y = x$.
*   This means that the $x$ and $y$ coordinates of the points on the graph of $y = \text{Cos } x$ are switched to obtain the points on the graph of $y = \text{Cos}^{-1}x$
*   **Reflection Symmetry:** $\begin{bmatrix} \text{Cos}^{-1}(-x) = \pi - \text{Cos}^{-1} x \text{ for } x \in [-1, 1] \\ (\text{Symmetric about the line } y = \frac{\pi}{2}) \end{bmatrix}$

    Symmetry along the range $[0, \pi]$ reflects across the vertical axis at $x = 0$.
*   **Inverse Function:** $\text{Cos}(\text{Cos}^{-1}x) = x \text{ for } x \in [-1, 1]$ and $\text{Cos}^{-1}(\text{Cos } x) = x \text{ for } x \in [0, \pi]$
