**Example 7:** Draw the graph of $y = -\text{Tan}^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) + \frac{\pi}{2}$ for $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

Identify the transformations and describe how they affect the graph of $y = \text{Tan}^{-1}x$.

**Solution:**

[Figure F1]

| $x$ | $y$ |
| :--- | :--- |
| $\pi$ | 0.6 |
| $\pi/2$. | 0.9 |
| $\pi/3$ | 1.1 |
| $\pi/4$ | 1.2 |

**1. Horizontal Stretch**

Argument of the Arctangent function is $\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)$. It results in a horizontal stretch by a factor 2.
*   **Effect:** The graph will stretch along $x$-axis, making it appear wider. It takes a larger value of $x$ to reach the same value of $y$ as in the standard graph of $y = \text{Tan}^{-1}(x)$.

**2. Reflection over $x$-axis**

Negative sign in front of Arctangent function $y = -\text{Tan}^{-1}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)$, reflects the graph over $x$-axis.
*   **Effect:** The entire graph is flipped vertically. At where the standard graph of $y = \text{Tan}^{-1}(x)$ increases, this graph decreases.

**3. Vertical Translation**

The term $+\frac{\pi}{2}$ indicates a vertical shift upwards by $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
*   **Effect:** The graph is shifted upwards by $\frac{\pi}{2}$ units, moving the entire curve up on $y$-axis.

**4. Combined Effect on the Graph**
*   **Shape:** The graph retains the basic shape of the Arctangent function, but due to the horizontal stretch, reflection, and vertical shift, it looks significantly different.
*   **Critical Points:** The graph approaches $\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ as $x$ approaches $(-\infty)$ and the graph approaches $\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$ as $x$ approaches $(+\infty)$, due to the reflection and vertical shift.
