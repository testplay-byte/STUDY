# Page 6 — Unit 09: Solution of Trigonometric Equations

> 📄 Original scan: [0006.jpg](../../../Raw/Mathematics/Unit-09-Solution-of-Trigonometric-Equations/0006.jpg) · printed page 267

When solving equations graphically, the intersection points of different functions correspond to the solutions of the equations. These graphical solutions can be approximated, especially when an exact algebraic solution is difficult or impossible to find. For example, finding the roots of a trigonometric equation like $\sin x = \cos x$ becomes straight forward by observing where the sine and cosine curves intersect.

Many trigonometric equations have multiple solutions within a given interval. Graphs make it easier to identify all these solutions visually.

Graphs bridge the gap between algebraic expressions and geometric interpretation. This connection helps in building an intuitive understanding of mathematical concepts, making it easier to grasp the underlying principles.

> Graphical solution is a powerful tool for approximating solutions, understanding the nature of trigonometric equations.

By following the above structure, one can cover the essential aspects of solving trigonometric equations graphically, providing both theoretical understanding and practical examples.

**Example 5:**

Solve graphically the trigonometric equation:
$$\sin \theta = \frac{\theta}{2} \text{ where } \theta \in \left[-\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{\pi}{2}\right]$$

**Solution:** In order to solve a trigonometric equation graphically, we follow as:
