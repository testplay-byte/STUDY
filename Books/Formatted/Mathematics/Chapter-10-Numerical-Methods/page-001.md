# NUMERICAL METHODS

**After studying this unit, students will be able to:**

*   Analyze the searching of roots of an equation by graphical means.
*   Explain the basic principles of solving a nonlinear equation in one variable.
*   Calculate real roots of a nonlinear equation in one variable by:
    *   Bisection Method
    *   Newton Raphson Method
    *   Regula Falsi Method
*   Demonstrate trapezium rule to estimate the value of a definite integral.
*   Apply concepts of nonlinear equations to real world problems.

The origins of modern numerical analysis are often linked to a 1947 paper by John von Neumann and Herman Goldstine, but others consider modern numerical analysis to go back to work by E. T. Whittaker in 1912.

Numerical methods are computational techniques designed to provide approximate solutions to mathematical problems that lack exact analytical solutions or are too complex to solve directly. They are widely used in science, engineering, and finance to handle problems involving differential equations, integrals, optimization, and systems of equations. Key methods include the Newton-Raphson algorithm for finding roots, the trapezoidal and Simpson’s rules for numerical integration, and finite difference methods for solving differential equations. These methods rely on breaking down problems into simpler, iterative steps, yielding increasingly accurate approximations. The precision of numerical methods can be enhanced by increasing the number of subintervals, iterations, or using higher-order algorithms, making them indispensable for handling real-world applications where exact solutions are impractical.
