## 10.3 Computers and Numerical Methods

Computers are ideally suited for numerical methods due to their ability to perform large numbers of calculations quickly and accurately. They can handle the repetitive and intensive computations required by numerical algorithms, making it possible to solve problems that would be infeasible by hand. As computer technology advances, so too does the efficiency and accuracy of numerical methods, allowing for the solution of increasingly complex problems in science and engineering.

In summary, numerical methods are indispensable in modern science and engineering, providing the tools necessary to approximate solutions to problems that cannot be solved analytically. As computational power continues to grow, so does the potential of these methods to solve even more challenging and complex problems.

## 10.4 Basic Principle of Solving Non-linear Equations

### i. Understanding Roots of Equations

*   **Roots or Zeros:** The roots (or zeros) of an equation $f(x) = 0$ are the values of $x$ that make the function equal to zero. For instance, if $f(x) = x^2 + 5x + 6$, the equation $f(x) = 0$, has roots at $x = -2$ and $x = -3$.

### ii. Types of Functions:

*   **Linear Functions:**
    *   **Form:** $f(x) = ax + b$
    *   **Solution Method:** Finding the roots of linear equations is straight forward and can be done using direct or analytical methods.

*   **Non-linear Functions:**
    *   **Algebraic Functions:**
        Form: Polynomial functions like $f(x)$ of degree n in the form:
        $$f(x) = a_n x_n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + a_{n-2} x^{n-2} + \ldots a_0$$
        *   **Degree:** For polynomials of degree higher than four, finding roots using direct methods becomes complex and often impractical.
    
    *   **Transcendental Functions:**
        Definition: Functions that involve non-algebraic operations like trigonometric, exponential, or logarithmic terms, such as:
        $$e^x + \sin(x) + 4 = 0, \quad e^x + \ln(x) - 7 = 0, \quad \tan(x) + x^3 + x^2 + 10 = 0$$
