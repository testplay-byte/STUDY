# GLOSSARY

**Absolute maximum:** A number $f(c)$ is an absolute maximum of a function $f$ if $f(x) \leq f(c)$ for every $x$ in the domain of $f$.

**Absolute minimum:** A number $f(c)$ is an absolute minimum of a function $f$ if $f(x) \geq f(c)$ for every $x$ in the domain of $f$.

**Algebraic function:** An algebraic function is a function that involves only algebraic operations.

**Anti-derivative:** A function $F$ is called an anti-derivative of another function $f(x)$ is on a given interval if $$F'(x) = f(x)$$

**Approximate solutions:** When exact solutions are unattainable, numerical methods provide close approximations, which are often sufficient for practical purposes.

**Axis of conic:** The line which passes through the focus and is perpendicular to the directrix is called axis of conic and its equation is $y = 0$.

**Bijective function:** A function which is both onto and one-one is called a bijective function.

**Bisection method:** It is used to find the roots of an equation. It subdivides the interval in which the root of the equation lies.

**Centre of ellipse or hyperbola:** The midpoint of both the foci is called the centre of the ellipse or hyperbola.

**Chord of a conics:** A line segment with its end points on the conic is called chord of that conic.

**Codomain of function:** The codomain is the set of all possible outcomes of the given function.

**Concurrent lines:** Three or more lines that intersect at one common point are said to be concurrent lines.

**Conjugate axis of hyperbola:** The line passing through the centre of the hyperbola and is perpendicular to its focal axis is known as conjugate axis or imaginary axis.

**Continuity on an interval:** A function is said to be continuous on an interval if it is continuous at every number in the interval.

**Continuous function:** A function is said to be continuous at a number $a$ if:
(i) $f(a)$ is defined, (ii) $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exits, and (iii) $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$

**Covertices of ellipse:** The points where the ellipse cut its minor axis are known as co-vertices of the ellipse.

**Covertices of hyperbola:** The points lying on the conjugate axis and are equidistant from the centre are known as co-vertices of the hyperbola.

**Critical values:** A critical value of a function $f$ is a number in $c$ in its domain for which $f'(c) = 0$ or $f'(c)$ does not exist.

**Degree of differential equation:** The degree of the differential equation is the power of the highest order derivative in the equation.

**Derivative functions:** The function $f'$ defined by the formula $f'(x_0) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \frac{f(x_0+\Delta x)-f(x)}{\Delta x}$ is called the derivative of $f$ with respect to $x$.

**Differential:** The function $f'(x)\Delta x$ is called differential of the dependent variable $y$ and is denoted by $dy$, written as $dy = f'(x)\Delta x = f'(x)dx$

**Differential equation:** A differential equation is an equation containing one or more derivatives of an unknown function.

**Directrices:** There are two fixed lines corresponding to each foci lying outside of the ellipse and are perpendicular to the major axis at a specific distance $\frac{a}{e}$. These lines are known as directrices of the ellipse

**Displacement function:** A function which gives the displacement of moving particle at any time ‘$t$’ is known as displacement function.

**Domain of function:** The set of all possible values of independent variable which qualify as inputs to a function is known as the domain of the function.

**Eccentricity:** The ratio of distance of any point on the conic from its focus to its directrix is called eccentricity of that conic and is denoted by “$e$”.

**Ellipse:** It is the set of all the points in the plane such that the sum of the distances of each point from two fixed points (foci) in the plane remains same.
