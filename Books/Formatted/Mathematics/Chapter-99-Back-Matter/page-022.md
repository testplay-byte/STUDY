# Page 22 — Glossary

> 📄 Original scan: [0022.jpg](../../../Raw/Mathematics/Back-Matter/0022.jpg) · printed page 311

**Explicit and implicit solution:** A solution of a differential equation that can be written in the form $y = f(x)$ is said to be an explicit solution while a solution of the form $f(x,y) = 0$ is said to be an implicit solution.

**Explicit function:** A function in which the dependent variable is expressed solely in terms of the independent variable $\mathbf{x}$, namely $y = f(x)$ is said to be an explicit function.

**First order differential equation:** A differential equation containing first order derivative is called first order differential equation.

**Focal chord:** A chord of the conic which passes through its focus is called focal chord.

**Focal distance:** The distance of any point of the conic from the focus is called focal distance.

**General solution of DE:** The solution that contains as many arbitrary constants as the order of the differential equation is called a general solution.

**Homogeneous differential equations:** A differential equation of the form $M(x,y)dx + N(x, y)dy = 0$ is said to be homogeneous if both $M$ and $N$ are homogeneous functions of the same degree.

**Homogeneous function:** If a function $f$ has the property that $f(tx, ty) = t^n f(x,y)$ for some real number $n$, then $f$ is said to be a nomogenous function of degree $n$.

**Homogeneous linear equation:** An equation of the form $ax + by = 0$, is called a homogeneous linear equation in two variables.

**Horizontal line test:** It is used to determine whether a function is one-one when its graph is given.

**Horizontal shift:** A horizontal shift adds/subtracts a positive constant to/from every x-coordinate while leaving the y-coordinate unchanged.

**Hyperbola:** It is the set of all points in a plane such that the difference of the distances of each point from two fixed points (foci) in the plane is same.

**Implicit function:** A function in which the dependent variable cannot be expressed solely in terms of the independent variable $\mathbf{x}$, is said to be an implicit function.

**Injective function:** A function which is both into and one-one is called an injective function.

**Instantaneous velocity:** Let $s = f(t)$ be a function that gives the position of an object moving in a straight line. The instantaneous velocity at time $t_1$ is $V(t_1) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$ whenever the limit exists.

**Integrable function:** A function $f$ is said to be integrable on a finite closed interval $[a, b]$ if the limit $\lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \sum_{k=1}^{n} f(x_k)\Delta x_k$ exists and does not depend upon the choice of partitions or on the choice of the points $x_k$ in the subintervals.

**Into function:** A function $f$: A → B is said to be an into function if rang $(f) \neq B$.

**Inverse function:** The inverse of any function $f(x)$ is a function denoted by $f^{-1}(x)$ which reverses the effect of $f(x)$ and it undoes what $f(x)$ does.

**Inverse trigonometric functions:** They are the inverses of the basic trigonometric functions, such as sine, cosine, tangent, cotangent, secant, and cosecant. They are used to calculate an angle from a trigonometric ratio of that angle.

**Latus rectum of conic:** A focal chord of the conic which is parallel to its directrix is called latus rectum.

**Limit of a function:** A function is said to have a limit L at $a$ if it is possible to make the function arbitrarily close to L by choosing values closer and closer to $a$.

**Linear function:** The function of the form, $y = ax + b; a,b \in R \wedge a \neq 0$ is called linear.

**Logarithmic functions:** These functions are inverse of exponential functions.

**Major axis of ellipse:** The line which passes through both the foci of ellipse is called major axis of the ellipse.

**Minor axis of ellipse:** The line passing through the centre of the ellipse and is perpendicular to the major axis is known as minor axis of the ellipse.

**Modulus function:** A modulus function determines a number's magnitude regardless of its sign.

**Newton-Raphson method:** It is a numerical analysis algorithm that finds the roots of a real-valued function. It is used to find approximate solutions to equations that can't be solved using analytical methods.

**Non-homogeneous linear equation:** An equation of the form $ax + by + c = 0$, is called a non-homogeneous linear equation in two variables.

**Non-linear function:** A function that is not linear is called a non-linear function.
