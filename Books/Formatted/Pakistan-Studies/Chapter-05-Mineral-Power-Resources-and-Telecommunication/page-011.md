### Disadvantages

Wind energy also presents some disadvantages. Its intermittent and variable nature means that energy production can fluctuate daily and seasonally, posing challenges for ensuring a consistent power supply. The initial installation and infrastructure costs for wind turbines are high, requiring substantial capital investment. Wind farms require large land areas, which can lead to land use conflicts, especially in densely populated or agriculturally important regions. Additionally, wind turbines can be visually unappealing and produce noise, which may be a concern for nearby residents. There are also potential risks to wildlife, particularly birds and bats, which can experience habitat disruption and mortality due to turbine operations.

## Solar Energy

Pakistan is endowed with significant solar energy potential, notably in its arid and semi-arid regions. With an average of over 300 sunny days per year, places like Sindh, Balochistan, and parts of Punjab are excellent for generating solar electricity. Large-scale solar farms are being created in these locations, alongside smaller, decentralized solar projects for rural and off-grid people. The government has been encouraging solar energy through various measures and incentives, recognizing its potential to contribute considerably to the national energy mix and reduce dependence on imported fossil fuels.

[Figure F1]

Examples of solar power projects include Quaid-e-Azam Solar Park in Bahawalpur with an installed capacity of 100 MW, Bhalwal Solar Power Plant in Sargodha with an installed capacity of 90 MW, and Matiari Solar Power Plant in Sindh with an installed capacity of 60 MW.

> **Do You Know?**
>
> Pakistan has immense potential for solar energy, with the ability to generate over 2.9 million megawatts annually. The country receives sunlight for about 300 days a year, averaging around 5.5 kilowatt-hours per square meter per day, making it one of the most solar-rich regions in the world. Government of Pakistan has taken significant steps of converting its government buildings on solar energy and provision of solar energy for domestic users on subsidized rates.

### Advantages

The advantages of solar energy in Pakistan are enormous. Solar power is a clean and renewable energy source, meaning it creates electricity without generating greenhouse gases or pollutants, therefore helping to combat climate change and improve air quality. The quantity of sunlight in Pakistan makes solar energy a reliable and continuous power source, particularly in places with limited access to traditional energy infrastructure.
