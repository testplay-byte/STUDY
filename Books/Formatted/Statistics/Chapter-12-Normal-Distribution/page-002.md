## 12.3. PROPERTIES OF THE NORMAL DISTRIBUTION

(i) It is symmetrical about ordinate at $X = \mu$. It means that the central ordinate at $X = \mu$ divides the curve into two equal parts.

(ii) The arithmetic mean, median and mode coincide.

(iii) The lower and upper quartiles are equidistant from the mean and are at a distance of $0.6745\,\sigma$.

(iv) Mean deviation is $0.7979\,\sigma$ or $4/5\,\sigma$ ( approximately ).

(v) Semi-interquartile range or quartile deviation is equal to the probable error which is equal to $0.6745\sigma$ or $2/3\,\sigma$( approximately ).

(vi) The ordinate is highest at the mean $\mu$.

(vii) It has two points of inflection ( the points where the curvature changes its direction ) which lie at a distance of one $\sigma$ above the mean $\mu$ and one $\sigma$ below the mean $\mu$ that is $\mu - \sigma$ and $\mu + \sigma$.

(viii) The curve is asymptotic to the base line. It means that it continues to approach but never reaches the base line.

(ix) In normal curve, if nth moment is odd, the value of this odd moment will always be zero. This is because the normal curve is symmetrical and for symmetrical distribution sum of the positive deviations from $\mu$ will always be equal to the sum of the negative deviations from $\mu$ and thus will cancel out each other. If nth moment is even, we have the relation

$$\mu_{n} = \frac{n!}{(2)^{\frac{n}{2}} \left( \frac{n}{2} \right)!}\,\sigma^{n} \quad (\text{where n is even})$$

It follows that $\mu_2 = \sigma^2$, $\mu_4 = 3\sigma^4$, $\beta_1 = \frac{\mu_3^2}{\mu_2^3} = \gamma_1^2 = 0$, that is skewness is zero and $\beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2} = 3$, $\gamma_2 = 0$, that is normal curve has zero kurtosis. The normal distribution is also called mesokurtic.

(x) The total area under the normal curve is unity.

(xi) The area under the part of the normal curve that lies within:

$\mu \pm 0.6745\,\sigma$ covers $50\%$ area | $\mu \pm 1\,\sigma$ covers $68.27\%$ area  
$\mu \pm 2\,\sigma$ covers $95.45\%$ area | $\mu \pm 3\,\sigma$ covers $99.73\%$ area .

## 12.4. STANDARD NORMAL DISTRIBUTION

The properties of the normal curve permit us to define a standardized distribution in terms of the variable Z defined as $Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}$
