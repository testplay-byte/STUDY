**9. Properties of Normal Distribution**

(i) The mean, median and mode are equal and are located at the centre of the distribution.

(ii) The total area under the normal distribution curve is equal to 1 or 100%.

(iii) Mean Deviation $= 0.7979\sigma$ or $= \frac{4}{5}\sigma$ (approximately) and Quartile Deviation $= 0.6745\sigma$ or $= \frac{2}{3}\sigma$ (approximately)

(iv) The odd order moments about mean are all zero.

(v) The points of inflection of the normal curve lie at $\mu - \sigma$ and $\mu + \sigma$.

(vi) The curve is symmetric about the mean, its shape is the same on both sides of a vertical line passing through the centre.

(vii) Moment ratios $\beta_1 = 0$ and $\beta_2 = 3$.

(viii) Maximum ordinate $= \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}$

(ix) $Q_1 = \mu - 0.6745\sigma$ and $Q_3 = \mu + 0.6745\sigma$

(x) $\mu \pm \sigma = 0.6827 = 68.27\%$, $\mu \pm 2\sigma = 0.9545 = 95.45\%$ and $\mu \pm 3\sigma = 0.9973 = 99.73\%$.

**10. Standard Normal Distribution**

The distribution of a normal random variable with mean zero and standard deviation one is called a standard normal distribution.

A normal distribution that has a mean of zero and standard deviation of one is called the standard normal distribution. If Z is the standard normal random variable, then Z has the probability distribution

$$f(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{z^2}{2}} \text{ for } -\infty < z < +\infty.$$

**11. Importance of Standard Normal Curve**

The importance of standard normal curve lies in the fact that one has not to tabulate probability ( areas ) under the curve between two lines X = a and X = b for different value of $\mu$ and $\sigma$. Only one table of areas prepared for the standard normal curve is sufficient as for this curve, the mean = $\mu = 0$ and S.D. = $\sigma = 1$.

**12. Z-Score**

The Z-score can be defined as the number of standard deviation that a value, X is above or below the mean of the distribution. From the Z formula ( that is $Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma}$ where $\sigma \neq 0$ ), this is clear that if the value of X is less than the mean, the Z-score is negative. If the value of X is greater than the mean, the Z-score is positive and if the value of X is equal to the mean, the Z-score is zero.

**13. Properties of Standard Normal Distribution**

(i) For the standard normal distribution, the value of the mean is equal to zero and the value of the standard deviation is equal to one.

(ii) The total area under the standard normal curve is one.

(iii) The formula for the standard score:

$$Z = \frac{\text{Value} - \text{Mean}}{\text{Standard Deviation}} = \frac{\text{X} - \mu}{\sigma}, \quad \sigma \neq 0.$$

(iv) Most of the area under the standard normal curve lies between – 3 and + 3.

(v) The standard normal curve is symmetric about mean zero.

**14. Normal Frequency Distribution**

When we multiply the normal distribution by N, the number of sets or experiments, we get the normal frequency distribution. The equation of the normal frequency distribution is

$$Y = \frac{N}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}\right)^2} \text{ for } -\infty < X < +\infty ; \sigma > 0$$

$$-\infty < \mu < +\infty ; \ e = 2.7183 \text{ and } \pi = 3.1416.$$

**15. Normal Approximation to the Binomial Distribution**

When the number of trials becomes large evaluating the binomial probability function is difficult, we may want to approximate the binomial distribution. In cases where the number of trials n becomes sufficiently large and probability of success is close to 1/2, both $np \geq 5$ and $nq \geq 5$, the normal distribution provides an easy to use approximation of binomial probabilities. When using the normal approximation to the binomial distribution, we set $\mu = np$, $\sigma = \sqrt{npq}$ and $Z = \frac{X - np}{\sqrt{npq}}$ in the definition of the normal curve.
