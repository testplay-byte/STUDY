# Page 22 — Normal Distribution (Chapter 12)

> 📄 Original scan: [0022.jpg](../../../Raw/Statistics/Chapter-12-Normal-Distribution/0022.jpg) · printed page 144

**42.** A random variable X is normally distributed with $\mu = 70$ and $\sigma^2 = 25$. The third moment about arithmetic mean is:
(a) zero (b) less than zero (c) greater than zero (d) none of the above

**43.** The area to the left of $(\mu + \sigma)$ for a normal distribution is approximately equal to:
(a) 0.16 (b) 0.34 (c) 0.50 (d) 0.84

**44.** P( $\mu - \sigma < X < \mu + \sigma$ ) is equal to:
(a) 0.5000 (b) 0.6827 (c) 0.9545 (d) 0.9973

**45.** In a normal curve $\mu \pm 0.6745 \sigma$ covers:
(a) $50\%$ area (b) $68.27\%$ area (c) $95.45\%$ area (d) $99.73\%$ area

**46.** In a normal curve $\mu \pm 2\sigma$ covers:
(a) $50\%$ area (b) $68.27\%$ area (c) $95.45\%$ area (d) $99.73\%$ area

**47.** In X is $N ( \mu , \sigma^2 )$, the percentage of the area contained within the limits $\mu \pm 3\sigma$ is:
(a) $50\%$ (b) $68.27\%$ (c) $95.45\%$ (d) $99.73\%$

**48.** Most of the area under the normal curve with parameters $\mu$ and $\sigma$ lies between:
(a) $\mu - 0.5\sigma$ and $\mu + 0.5\sigma$ (b) $\mu - \sigma$ and $\mu + \sigma$
(c) $\mu - 2\sigma$ and $\mu + 2\sigma$ (d) $\mu - 3\sigma$ and $\mu + 3\sigma$.

**49.** For a normal distribution with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, approximately $5\%$ of values are:
(a) outside the range $(\mu - 2\sigma)$ to $(\mu + 2\sigma)$ (b) greater than $(\mu + 2\sigma)$
(c) outside the range $(\mu - \sigma)$ to $(\mu + \sigma)$ (d) less than $(\mu - 3\sigma)$

**50.** In a normal distribution $Q_1 = 20$ and $Q_3 = 40$, then mean is equal to:
(a) 20 (b) 30 (c) 40 (d) 60

**51.** Given a random variable X which is normally distributed with a mean and variance both equal to 100. The value of mean deviation is approximately equal to:
(a) 7 (b) 8 (c) 8.5 (d) 9

**52.** If X is a normal variate with mean 50 and standard deviation 3. The value of quartile deviation is approximately equal to:
(a) 1 (b) 1.5 (c) 2 (d) 2.5

**53.** In a normal distribution mean is 100 and standard deviation is 10. The values of points of inflection are:
(a) 100 and 110 (b) 80 and 120 (c) 90 and 110 (d) none of the above

**54.** If $X \sim N ( \mu , \sigma^2 )$, then $\mu_4$ is equal to:
(a) 3 (b) $3\sigma$ (c) $3\sigma^2$ (d) $3\sigma^4$

**55.** If $X \sim N ( \mu , \sigma^2 )$, then $\beta_2$ is equal to:
(a) 0 (b) 3 (c) $3\sigma^4$ (d) $\sigma^4/3$

**56.** If X is a normal variate with mean 20 and variance 16. The respective values of $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$ are:
(a) 0 and 3 (b) 3 and 1 (c) 0.5 and 1 (d) 3 and 3

**57.** If X is $N ( 100, 5 )$, the fourth central moment is:
(a) 65 (b) 75 (c) 85 (d) 100

**58.** For a normal distribution with $\mu = 10$, $\sigma = 2$, the probability of a value greater than 10 is:
(a) 0.1915 (b) 0.3085 (c) 0.6915 (d) 0.5000

**59.** Given a normal distribution with $\mu = 100$ and $\sigma^2 = 100$, the area to the left of 100 is:
(a) one (b) equal to 0.5 (c) less than 0.5 (d) greater than 0.5

**60.** If a normal distribution with $\mu = 200$ has $P( X > 225 ) = 0.1587$, then $P( X < 175 )$ equal to:
(a) 0.3413 (b) 0.8413 (c) 0.1587 (d) 0.5000

**61.** A normal distribution has the mean $\mu = 200$. If 70 percent of the area under the curve lies to the left of 220, the area to the right of 220 is:
(a) 0.3 (b) 0.5 (c) 0.2 (d) 0.7

**62.** A random variable has a normal distribution with the mean $\mu = 400$. If 80 percent of the area under the curve lies to the left of 500, the area between 400 and 500 is:
(a) 0.5 (b) 0.2 (c) 0.3 (d) zero
