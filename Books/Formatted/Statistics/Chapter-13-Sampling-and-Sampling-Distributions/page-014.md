**Example 13.7.**

(i) If the size of sample is 36 and standard error of mean is 2. Find standard error of the mean if the sample size is increased to 144.

(ii) If the size of the simple random sample from an infinite population is 55, the variance of sample mean is 27. What must be the standard error of sample mean if n = 165?

**Solution:** The necessary calculations are given below:

(i) Here, $n = 36$, $S.E (\bar{X}) = 2$ and $\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = 4$. Therefore

$$\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \quad \text{or} \quad 4 = \frac{\sigma^2}{36} \quad \text{or} \quad \sigma^2 = 4(36) = 144$$

Now if $n = 144$, then $S.E (\bar{X}) = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{144}{144}} = 1$

(ii) Here, $n = 55$ and $\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = 27$. Therefore

$$\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \quad \text{or} \quad 27 = \frac{\sigma^2}{55} \quad \text{or} \quad \sigma^2 = 55(27) = 1485$$

Now if $n = 165$, then $S.E (\bar{X}) = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{1485}{165}} = \sqrt{9} = 3$

**Example 13.8.**

A population has the values 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 and 20. Draw all possible samples of size 2 without replacement and calculate the sample mean $\bar{X}$ for each sample. Write the sampling distribution of $\bar{X}$. Find the following probabilities:

(i) $\bar{X}$ will be greater than 16.    (ii) $\bar{X}$ will differ from $\mu$ by less than 3 units.

(iii) Sampling error will be less than 2.    (iv) $\bar{X}$ will be equal to $\mu$.

**Solution:** All possible samples of size 2 will be equal to ${}^{6}\text{C}_{2} = \frac{6!}{2!\,4!} = 15$
