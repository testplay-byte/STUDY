# Page 32 — Sampling and Sampling Distributions (Chapter 13)

> 📄 Original scan: [0032.jpg](../../../Raw/Statistics/Chapter-13-Sampling-and-Sampling-Distributions/0032.jpg) · printed page 186

**19. Parameter**

A parameter is a numerical descriptive measure of a population. *or*

A parameter is any measure which describes a population.

**20. Statistic**

A statistic is quantity calculated from the observations in a sample. *or*

A measure computed on the basis of sample data is termed as statistic.

**21. Census**

The study of all the data points in a population is called a census. *or*

To study all the individual observations of the entire population is called census.

**22. Sample Survey**

A survey to collect data on a sample is called sample survey. *or*

If it is not essential to conduct the complete enumeration, then a sample of some suitable size is selected from the population and the study is carried out on the sample. This study is called sample survey. Most of the research work is done through sample survey.

**23. Basic Aims or Objects of Sampling**

(i) To get maximum information about a population without examining each and every unit of the population.

(ii) To find reliability of estimates derived from the sample.

**24. Advantages of Sampling**

(i) Sampling is cheaper than complete count.

(ii) The data are collected and analyzed more quickly.

(iii) Sampling saves time.

(iv) A higher quality of labour with better supervision can be employed due to reduced volume of material.

(v) A small fraction of population gives sometimes comprehensive and detailed results.

**25. Demerits or Limitations of Sampling**

(i) Sometimes a complete count is necessary and sampling is not allowed. For example, to conduct the elections, we need a complete list of the voters. Tax is collected from all the tax payers. The telephone, gas and electricity bills are sent to all the consumers etc.

(ii) It is difficult to decide which method of sampling is the most suitable. Sample is a part of population, the result of sample is less reliable than complete count.

**26. Sample Design**

A sample design is a definite pre-determined plan of a sequence of steps formulated in advance in the selection of samples and inference.

**27. Sampling Design**

A sampling design is a definite statistical plan which has all steps taken in the selection of the sample and method of estimation. *or*

The sampling design specifies the method of collecting the sample.

**28. Sampling Frame**

A sampling frame is a list of all sampling units in the population. *or*

A list of the sampling units for a study is called a sampling frame.

**29. Probability sampling**

A probability sampling is one in which the sampling units are chosen on the basis of known probabilities. *or*

When each and every element of the population has known probability of being selected in the sample, then sampling is said to be probability sampling.

**30. Major Probability Sampling Techniques**

(i) Simple random sampling

(ii) Stratified random sampling

(iii) Systematic random sampling

(iv) Cluster sampling

**31. Non-Probability Sampling**

The sampling is said to be non-probability sampling when the procedure of selecting the elements from the population is not based on probability but personal judgement is involved in selection.

**32. Major Non-probability Sampling Techniques**

(i) Purposive sampling

(ii) Quota sampling

**33. Sampling with Replacement**

When an object is selected from the population and is replaced before the next object is selected, such a selection is known as sampling with replacement. *or*

Sampling is said to be with replacement when we draw a sampling unit from a population and return it to the population before the next unit is drawn. In sampling with replacement, an element can be chosen more than once in a sample.
