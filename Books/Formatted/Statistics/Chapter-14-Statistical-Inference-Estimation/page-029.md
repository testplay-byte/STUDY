# Page 29 — Statistical Inference Estimation (Chapter 14)

> 📄 Original scan: [0029.jpg](../../../Raw/Statistics/Chapter-14-Statistical-Inference-Estimation/0029.jpg) · printed page 231

**74.** If we have normal populations with known population standard deviations $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$, the confidence interval estimate for the difference between two population means $(\mu_1 - \mu_2)$ is:
(a) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{S_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{S_2^2}{n_2}}$  (b) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}$
(c) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2}{n_1+n_2}}$  (d) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2 \sigma_2^2}{n_1 n_2}}$

**75.** If the population standard deviations $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ are unknown and sample sizes $n_1, n_2 > 30$, the $100(1-\alpha)\%$ confidence interval for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$ is:
(a) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{S_1^2+S_2^2}{n_1+n_2}}$  (b) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{S_1^2 S_2^2}{n_1 n_2}}$
(c) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{S_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{S_2^2}{n_2}}$  (d) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}$

**76.** If $n_1 = 16, n_2 = 9$ and $\alpha = 0.01; t_{\frac{\alpha}{2}}$ equals:
(a) 2.787  (b) 2.807  (c) 2.797  (d) 3.767

**77.** If $n_1, n_2 \leq 30$, the confidence interval estimate for the difference of two population means $(\mu_1 - \mu_2)$ when population standard deviations $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ are unknown but equal in case of pooled variates is:
(a) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{S_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{S_2^2}{n_2}}$  (b) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm t_{\frac{\alpha}{2}(v)} \sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}$
(c) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm t_{\frac{\alpha}{2}(v)} s_p \sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1}+\frac{1}{n_2}}$  (d) $(\bar{X}_1-\bar{X}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_1^2}{n_1}+\frac{\sigma_2^2}{n_2}}$

The confidence interval estimate for the difference of two population means $\mu_1 - \mu_2 = \mu_d$ in case of paired observations small sample $(n \leq 30)$ is:
(a) $\bar{X} \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}$  (b) $\bar{d} \pm t_{\frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)} \frac{s_d}{\sqrt{n}}$  (c) $\bar{d} \pm t_{\frac{\alpha}{2}(n-1)} \sqrt{\frac{s_1^2+s_2^2}{n}}$  (d) $b \pm t_{\frac{\alpha}{2}(n-2)} s_b$

**79.** If the sample size is large, the confidence interval estimate of a population proportion p is:
(a) $\bar{X} \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \frac{S}{\sqrt{n}}$  (b) $\hat{p} \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}\hat{q}}{n}}$  (c) $\hat{p} \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}+\hat{q}}{n}}$  (d) $\hat{p} \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{n\hat{p}\hat{q}}$

**80.** If the sample size is large, the confidence interval estimate for the difference between two population proportions $p_1 - p_2$ is:
(a) $(\hat{p}_1-\hat{p}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_1\hat{q}_1}{n_1}+\frac{\hat{p}_2\hat{q}_2}{n_2}}$  (b) $(\hat{p}_1-\hat{p}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_1\hat{q}_1+\hat{p}_2\hat{q}_2}{n_1+n_2}}$
(c) $(\hat{p}_1-\hat{p}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\hat{p}_1\hat{q}_1+\hat{p}_2\hat{q}_2}$  (d) $(\hat{p}_1-\hat{p}_2) \pm Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} \sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}_1\hat{q}_1+\hat{p}_2\hat{q}_2}{n_1 n_2}}$
