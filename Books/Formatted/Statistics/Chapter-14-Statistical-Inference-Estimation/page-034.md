# Page 34 — Statistical Inference Estimation (Chapter 14)

> 📄 Original scan: [0034.jpg](../../../Raw/Statistics/Chapter-14-Statistical-Inference-Estimation/0034.jpg) · printed page 236

**Q.22** A manufacturing company consists of two departments producing identical products. It is suspected that the hourly outputs in the two departments are different. Two random samples of production hours are respectively selected and the following data are obtained:

| | Department 1 | Department 2 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sample size | 64 | 49 |
| Sample mean | 100 | 90 |

The variances of the hourly outputs for the two departments are known to be $\sigma_1^2 = 256$ and $\sigma_2^2 = 196$ respectively. Find the 96% confidence limits for the true difference.

**Ans.** $4.19 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < 15.81$

**Q.23** A standardized statistics test was given to 75 boys and 50 girls. The boys made an average grade of 82 with a variance of 64, while the girls made an average grade of 76 with a variance of 36. Find a 99% confidence interval for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$, where $\mu_1$ stands for mean score of all boys and $\mu_2$ stands for mean score of all girls.

**Ans.** $2.77 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < 9.23$

**Q.24** Let two independent random samples each of size 100, from independent normal distributions $N(\mu_1, \sigma_1^2)$ and $N(\mu_2, \sigma_2^2)$ yield $\bar{X}_1 = 4.8$, $S_1^2 = 8.64$, $\bar{X}_2 = 5.6$ and $S_2^2 = 7$. Find 95% confidence interval for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$.

**Ans.** $- 1.58 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < - 0.02$

**Q.25** Two independent random samples of the diameters of tires are drawn, one from a batch of tires produced at plant A, and another from a batch of tires produced at plant B. The results are as follows:

| Sample | Sample size | Sample mean (inches) | Sample variance (inches)$^2$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Plant A | 100 | 50.7 | 0.09 |
| Plant B | 100 | 50.3 | 0.04 |

Calculate a 95 % confidence interval for the difference between the mean diameter of the entire batch produced at plant A and the mean diameter of the entire batch produced at plant B.

**Ans.** $0.33 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < 0.47$

**Q.26** A sample of 150 brand A light bulbs showed a mean lifetime of 1400 hours with a standard deviation of 120 hours. A sample of 200 brand B light bulbs showed a mean lifetime of 1200 hours with a standard deviation of 80 hours. Compute 95% and 99% confidence limits for the difference of the mean lifetime of the population of brands A and B.

**Ans.** $177.83 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < 222.17$ and $170.87 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < 229.13$

**Q.27** Given $n_1 = 48$, $\bar{X}_1 = 90$, $n_2 = 72$, $\bar{X}_2 = 85$. $S_1^2 = 12$, $S_2^2 = 18$ and $Z_{\frac{\alpha}{2}} = 1.96$. Find 95% confidence interval for $\mu_1 - \mu_2$.

**Ans.** $3.61 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < 6.39$

**Q.28** Urban and rural students are to be compared on the basis of their scores on a nationwide musical aptitude test. Two random samples of sizes 100 and 90 respectively are selected from urban and rural seventh-grade students. The summary statistics from the test scores are:

| | Urban | Rural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sample size | 100 | 90 |
| Mean | 81 | 76 |
| Standard Deviation | 7 | 8 |

Compute a 98% confidence interval for the difference in population mean scores between urban and rural students.

**Ans.** $2.45 < \mu_1 - \mu_2 < 7.55$
