## MULTIPLE – CHOICE QUESTIONS

**1.** A statement about a population developed for the purpose of testing is called:
(a) hypothesis (b) hypothesis testing (c) level of significance (d) test-statistic

**2.** To check the validity of a statement about a population parameter is called:
(a) test statistic (b) significance test (c) power of test (d) testing of hypothesis

**3.** Any statement whose validity is tested on the basis of a sample is called:
(a) null hypothesis (b) alternative hypothesis (c) statistical hypothesis (d) simple hypothesis

**4.** A quantitative statement about a population is called:
(a) research hypothesis (b) composite hypothesis (c) simple hypothesis (d) statistical hypothesis

**5.** Any hypothesis which is tested for the purpose of rejection under the assumption that it is true is called:
(a) null hypothesis (b) alternative hypothesis (c) statistical hypothesis (d) composite hypothesis

**6.** A null hypothesis is denoted by:
(a) $H_0$ (b) $H_1$ (c) $H_A$ (d) $H_B$

**7.** A statement about the value of a population parameter is called:
(a) null hypothesis (b) alternative hypothesis (c) simple hypothesis (d) composite hypothesis

**\***
Accepting a null hypothesis $H_0$:
(a) proves that $H_0$ is true (b) proves that $H_0$ is false
(c) implies that $H_0$ is likely to be true (d) proves that $\mu \leq 0$.

Which of the following cannot be null hypothesis $H_0$:
(a) $\mu \leq \mu_0$ (b) $\mu \geq \mu_0$ (c) $\mu = \mu_0$ (d) $\mu \neq \mu_0$

**10.** A statement that is accepted if the sample data provide sufficient evidence that the null hypothesis is false is called:
(a) simple hypothesis (b) composite hypothesis (c) statistical hypothesis (d) alternative hypothesis

**11.** The alternative hypothesis is denoted by:
(a) $H_0$ (b) $H_1$ (c) $H_A$ (d) both (b) and (c)

**12.** The alternative hypothesis is also called:
(a) null hypothesis (b) statistical hypothesis (c) research hypothesis (d) simple hypothesis

**13.** Which hypothesis is always in an inequality form?
(a) null hypothesis (b) alternative hypothesis (c) simple hypothesis (d) composite hypothesis

**14.** When $H_0: \mu \geq \mu_0$, then $H_1$ is:
(a) $\mu > \mu_0$ (b) $\mu < \mu_0$ (c) $\mu \neq \mu_0$ (d) $\mu \leq \mu_0$

**15.** If $H_0 : \mu = \mu_0$, then $H_1$ is:
(a) $\mu < \mu_0$ (b) $\mu > \mu_0$ (c) $\mu \neq \mu_0$ (d) $\mu \leq \mu_0$

**16.** When $H_0: \mu \leq \mu_0$, then:
(a) $H_1: \mu > \mu_0$ (b) $H_1: \mu \neq \mu_0$ (c) $H_1: \mu \geq \mu_0$ (d) $H_1: \mu < \mu_0$

**17.** Any hypothesis which is accepted when null hypothesis is rejected is called:
(a) research hypothesis (b) alternative hypothesis (c) statistical hypothesis (d) both (a) and (b)

**18.** The complement of the null hypothesis is called:
(a) simple hypothesis (b) composite hypothesis
(c) statistical hypothesis (d) alternative hypothesis

**19.** A hypothesis that specifies all the values of parameter is called:
(a) simple hypothesis (b) composite hypothesis (c) statistical hypothesis (d) none of the above.
