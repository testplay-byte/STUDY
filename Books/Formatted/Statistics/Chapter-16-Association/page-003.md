# Page 3 — Association (Chapter 16)

The classes A, $\alpha$, B and $\beta$ are classes of the order one and the frequencies (A), ($\alpha$), (B) and ($\beta$) are the frequencies of order one. The classes AB, A$\beta$, $\alpha$B and $\alpha\beta$ are the classes of order two and the frequencies (AB), (A$\beta$), ($\alpha$B) and ($\alpha\beta$) are the frequencies of order two. The classes **ABC**, **AB$\gamma$**, **$\alpha$BC**, **$\alpha$B$\gamma$**, **A$\beta$C**, **A$\beta\gamma$**, **$\alpha\beta$C** and **$\alpha\beta\gamma$** are the classes of order three and (**ABC**), (**AB$\gamma$**) ... (**$\alpha\beta\gamma$**) are the frequencies of order three. The sample size n does not contain any attribute and is therefore called frequency of order zero.

## 16.7. ULTIMATE CLASS FREQUENCIES

In a certain given situation, the *ultimate class frequencies* are the frequencies with the highest order. For two attributes, the ultimate class frequencies are (AB), (A$\beta$), ($\alpha$B) and ($\alpha\beta$).

For three attributes, the ultimate class frequencies are of order 3 which are (**ABC**), (**AB$\gamma$**), (**$\alpha$BC**), (**$\alpha$B$\gamma$**), (**A$\beta$C**), (**A$\beta\gamma$**) (**$\alpha\beta$C**) and (**$\alpha\beta\gamma$**).

## 16.8. LOWER ORDER FREQUENCIES IN TERMS OF HIGHER ORDER FREQUENCIES

Let us discuss the relation of the lower order frequencies in terms of higher order frequencies.
Let us consider the different cases.

**(i)** Single Attribute

$$n = (\text{A}) + (\alpha)$$

**(ii)** Two Attributes

Let us consider $2 \times 2$ contingency Table-2. for the frequencies of the two attributes. Clearly

$$\begin{array}{ll}
n = (\text{A}) + (\alpha) & \quad n = (\text{B}) + (\beta) \\
(\text{A}) = (\text{AB}) + (\text{A}\beta) & \quad (\alpha) = (\alpha\text{B}) + (\alpha\beta) \\
(\text{B}) = (\text{AB}) + (\alpha\text{B}) & \quad (\beta) = (\text{A}\beta) + (\alpha\beta)
\end{array}$$

**(iii)** Three Attributes

Let us take help from Table-3. to write lower order frequencies into higher order frequencies.
Clearly

$$\begin{aligned}
n &= (\text{A}) + (\alpha) \quad \text{also} \quad n = (\text{B}) + (\beta) \\
(\text{A}) &= (\text{AC}) + (\text{A}\gamma). \text{ Here } (\text{AC})=(\text{ABC})+(\text{A}\beta\text{C}) \text{ and } (\text{A}\gamma)=(\text{AB}\gamma)+(\text{A}\beta\gamma). \textbf{Therefore} \\
(\text{A}) &= (\text{ABC})+(\text{A}\beta\text{C})+(\text{AB}\gamma)+(\text{A}\beta\gamma) \\
\text{Similarly } (\alpha) &= (\alpha\text{C})+(\alpha\gamma) \quad = (\alpha\text{BC})+(\alpha\beta\text{C})+(\alpha\text{B}\gamma)+(\alpha\beta\gamma) \\
n &= (\text{A})+(\alpha) \quad = (\text{ABC})+(\text{A}\beta\text{C})+(\text{AB}\gamma)+(\text{A}\beta\gamma)+(\alpha\text{BC})+(\alpha\beta\text{C})+(\alpha\text{B}\gamma)+(\alpha\beta\gamma) \\
(\text{B})&=(\text{AB})+(\alpha\text{B}) \quad = (\text{ABC})+(\text{AB}\gamma)+(\alpha\text{BC})+(\alpha\text{B}\gamma) \\
(\beta)&=(\text{A}\beta)+(\alpha\beta) \quad = (\text{A}\beta\text{C})+(\text{A}\beta\gamma)+(\alpha\beta\text{C})+(\alpha\beta\gamma) \\
n &= (\text{ABC})+(\text{AB}\gamma)+(\alpha\text{BC})+(\alpha\text{B}\gamma)+(\text{A}\beta\text{C})+(\text{A}\beta\gamma)+(\alpha\beta\text{C})+(\alpha\beta\gamma)
\end{aligned}$$

With the help of the Table-2 and Table-3 we can easily write any lower order frequency in terms of higher orders.

## 16.9. HIGHER ORDER FREQUENCIES INTO LOWER ORDER FREQUENCIES

Sometimes we have to express the frequency of a higher order into frequencies of lower order. For this purpose we use the following operators. The frequency (A) is written as n · A as if n · A means A's out of n. Similarly the frequency ($\alpha$) is written as n . $\alpha$ and (AB) is written as n . AB and (**ABC**) is written as n . ABC.

We know $(\text{A}) + (\alpha) = \text{n}$

$$\text{n} \cdot \text{A} + \text{n} \cdot \alpha = \text{n} \qquad \dots\dots \quad \text{equation (1)}$$

Using the operators, we shall assume that algebraic operations are applicable on these operators.
Dividing equation (1) by n, we get

$$\text{A}+\alpha = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad \text{A} = 1-\alpha \quad \text{and} \quad \alpha = 1-\text{A}$$

Similarly we can establish with the help of operators that

$$\text{B} = 1-\beta \text{ and } \beta = 1-\text{B} \quad \text{also} \quad \text{C} = 1-\gamma \text{ and } \gamma = 1-\text{C}$$
