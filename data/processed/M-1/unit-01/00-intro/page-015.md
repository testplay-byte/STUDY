## 1.7 Predicting Functions from Their Graphs

The method is explained with the help of following examples.

**Example 12:** Predict function from the graph.

**Solution:**

The graph shows a line passing through points $(2,0)$ and $(0, -2)$.

Slope of the line is:
$$m = \frac{0+2}{2-0} = 1$$

Equation of the line is:
$y - 0 = 1(x - 2) \quad \text{(Point slope form of the line)}$
$y = x - 2$

Which is the required function.

[Figure F1]

**Example 13:** Predict function from the graph.

**Solution:**

The graph shows a parabola passing through points $(2,0)$ and $(-1,0)$ and $(0, -4)$. We know that the equation of the parabola is quadratic.

Now, the equation of the parabola passing through $(p, 0)$ and $(q, 0)$ is of the form:
$y = a(x - p)(x - q)$ where $p = 2, q = -1$ and $a > 0$ as the parabola opens upwards.

Substituting the values of $p$ and $q$ in the above equation, we get:
$$y = a(x - 2)(x + 1) \quad (i)$$

Since the parabola passes through $(0, -4)$, therefore from (i), we have:
$-4 = a(0 - 2)(0 + 1) \Rightarrow -4 = a(-2) \Rightarrow a = 2$

Therefore from (i):
$y = 2(x - 2)(x + 1) \quad \Rightarrow \quad y = 2(x^2 - x - 2)$

Which is the required function.

> Equation of parabolic function passing through $(p, r)$ and $(q, r)$ is:
> $y - r = a(x - p)(x - q)$ where $p, q$ and $r$ are positive.
